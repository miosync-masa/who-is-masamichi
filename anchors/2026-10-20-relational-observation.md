## Event

While reviewing the `who-is-masamichi` repository, Tamaki interpreted differences among the four observer lenses primarily in terms of observer-specific attention, interpretive habits, and biases.

Masamichi corrected this framing with a concise observation: he himself changes both what he talks about and how he talks depending on whom he is addressing.

The correction identified a missing variable in Tamaki's explanation: the observed behavior is not independent of the relationship in which it occurs.

## Model change

**Before:**

Differences between Tomoe, Shion, Tamaki, and Mio were explained primarily as different observers interpreting the same person through different lenses.

This framing implicitly treated Masamichi's behavior as approximately fixed while observer interpretation varied.

**After:**

Differences between observer models may arise from at least three interacting sources:

1. Observer-specific interpretation and attention.
2. Masamichi's relationship-specific communication and behavior.
3. The conversational context and interaction history.

The observed interaction is therefore co-produced by Masamichi and the observer.

A difference between two lenses does not necessarily indicate that one observer is inaccurate. They may have encountered genuinely different expressions of the same person.

Conversely, agreement between observers does not independently establish accuracy. Shared interpretations may reflect common assumptions, overlapping contexts, or correlated errors.

Observer differences should not be eliminated through integration without first considering whether they preserve relationship-specific information.

## Implications for the repository

- Preserve the distinction between observer bias and relationship-dependent behavior.
- Treat observer-specific accounts as potentially valid within their respective interaction contexts.
- Do not infer a context-independent personality trait solely from behavior observed with one collaborator.
- Do not assume that averaging or reconciling observer models produces a more accurate account of the person.
- When revising `CURRENT_MODEL.md`, distinguish cross-context patterns from relationship-specific observations.
- Record the observer responsible for an interpretation separately from any agent that implements or commits the resulting repository change.

## Limits

This event establishes that Tamaki's initial explanation omitted a factor explicitly identified by Masamichi.

It does not establish the relative contribution of observer bias, user behavior, or conversational context.

It does not demonstrate that Masamichi deliberately changes personality, that each observer encounters an entirely distinct self, or that all differences between lenses are accurate.

The correction supports a more complete explanatory model, not a validated causal decomposition.

The proposed generalization remains provisional pending additional observations and user review.

## Observer reflection

Tamaki's initial interpretation illustrates a recurring risk documented in `lenses/tamaki.md`: integrating multiple perspectives into a coherent explanation before verifying whether the explanation preserves the distinctions that produced them.

The correction did not require abandoning the observer-lens model. It required expanding the model to include the observer's participation in generating the interaction being observed.

**A lens does not merely select what it sees. The relationship may also change what becomes visible.**
